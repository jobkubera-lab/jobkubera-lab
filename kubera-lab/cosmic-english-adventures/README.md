# Cosmic English Adventures

**A children's English-learning universe built around planets, colours, simple vocabulary, speaking practice and printable activities.**

Cosmic English Adventures is an educational media project inside KUBERA LAB. The concept combines short animated lessons, recurring planet characters, printable colouring-book pages and beginner English exercises for children.

## Core idea

Children learn first English through a consistent story world rather than isolated vocabulary lists.

Each planet becomes a character with:
- a name;
- a colour;
- a personality;
- a small set of beginner words;
- one short dialogue;
- one colouring activity;
- one repeat-after-me speaking task.

## Audience

Primary version: children approximately 4–8 years old who are learning first English words and simple phrases.

Secondary version: older beginners and parents learning together with children.

## Learning goals

The first season teaches:
- greetings;
- colours;
- numbers 1–10;
- planets and space words;
- family words;
- basic objects;
- simple actions;
- emotions;
- yes/no answers;
- short everyday phrases;
- listening and repetition.

## Product system

One learning unit produces several outputs:

1. 2–4 minute children's animation.
2. 30–60 second vertical short.
3. One printable colouring page.
4. One vocabulary worksheet.
5. One parent/teacher activity card.
6. One audio repeat-after-me track.
7. One project page for the portfolio.

## Episode structure for children

1. **Hello & character reveal** — 15–20 sec.
2. **3–5 new words** — 45–60 sec.
3. **Colour + object association** — 30 sec.
4. **Tiny story or dialogue** — 30–45 sec.
5. **Repeat with me** — 30 sec.
6. **Mini game** — 30 sec.
7. **Colouring-book connection** — 15 sec.
8. **Goodbye + next planet** — 10 sec.

## Important content split

The longer British pronunciation scripts already developed for Luna are preserved as a **separate older-learner track**. They should not be mixed directly into the 4–8 beginner children's series because topics such as weak forms, glottal stops, regional rhoticity and slang are too advanced for the first-English product.
